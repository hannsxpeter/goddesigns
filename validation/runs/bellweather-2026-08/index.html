The pan log

Every pond and pan on the flat, read once a day. Concentrators feed the crystallizers; a pan is rakeable between about 25 and 29 degrees Baume.

0.5 to 2 mm

Flake, skimmed

The crust that forms on still brine and is lifted off the surface before it sinks. Hollow, pyramidal, and it snaps between the fingers. This is the salt you put on at the pass, not in the pot.

2 to 4 mm

Coarse, raked

Raked off the pan floor once the flake has been taken. Denser, faintly grey from the clay, and slower to dissolve. It holds up on roasting vegetables and in a brine.

Both grades are drained but never kiln dried, so they carry 2 to 4 percent moisture at pack and are sold by drained weight. Salt that has been dried hard has usually been washed first, and washing is what takes the taste out.

Visits

Two hours on the flat, at low tide.

April to September, tides permitting. You walk the concentrators, read a hydrometer yourself, and rake a pan. Boots are supplied and the flat is soft going, so it is not a step-free route.
